# Scenario patterns

## Single experimental paper
Highlight problem definition, which information the authors use, proposed mechanism and training setup, datasets/splits, principal figure(s), meaningful ablations, failures, limitations and discussion. Do not present a main-method figure without the input and output semantics needed to read it.

## Multiple papers / reading report
Construct a real comparison dimension first (task, data, representation, operation, protocol, result). Acknowledge irreducible metric/protocol differences. Never imply three independent papers are components in one common architecture just because they use related terms.

## Review / survey
Create a taxonomy and evidence table. Attribute claims. Avoid inventing an experiment for a survey or treating a cited paper's figure as the review's own result.

## Theory
Introduce definitions and assumptions, central statement and interpretation, one worked example, proof intuition and applicability. Do not promise a quantitative superiority figure that the source does not contain.

## Teaching
Prefer an explicit example first, progressive conceptual explanation, one worked problem, and an accurate recap. Clearly mark synthetic examples as such.

## Proposal
Separate established prior evidence, an explicit testable hypothesis, planned methods, controls/metrics, deliverables and risks. Proposed sample sizes or targets must not be written as completed experiment outcomes.
